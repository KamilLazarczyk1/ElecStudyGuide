DC Basics Lecture 1

Coulomb's Law

F = \frac{k Q_{1}Q_{2}}{r^{2}}
  • F – force between charges (N)
  • k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
  • Q₁, Q₂ – charges (C); r – distance (m)

Attraction for opposite charges, repulsion for like charges.

Voltage (potential difference)

V = \frac{W}{Q}
  • V – voltage (V); 1 V = 1 J/C
  • W – energy (J); Q – charge (C)

Current

I = \frac{Q}{t}
  • I – current (A); 1 A = 1 C/s
  • Q – charge (C); t – time (s)
  • 1 C = charge of 6.242 × 10¹⁸ electrons

Resistance of a conductor

R = \rho \frac{l}{A}
  • R – resistance (Ω)
  • ρ – resistivity of the material
  • l – length; A – cross-section area

Longer → more R; thicker → less R.

Temperature effect on R

R_{1} = R_{20}[1 + \alpha _{20}(T_{1} - 20^{\circ}\text{C})]
  • R₂₀ – resistance at 20 °C (Ω)
  • α₂₀ – temperature coefficient (1/°C)
  • T₁ – new temperature (°C)

Conductors: R rises with temperature.

PPM temperature sensitivity

\Delta R = \frac{R_{\text{nominal}}}{10^{6}} (\text{PPM})(\Delta T)
  • ΔR – resistance change (Ω)
  • PPM – rating in parts-per-million/°C
  • ΔT – temperature change (°C)

Conductance

G = \frac{1}{R}
  • G – conductance (siemens, S)
  • R – resistance (Ω)

Ohm's Law

I = \frac{E}{R}\quad \Leftrightarrow \quad E = I R
  • I – current (A)
  • E – applied voltage (V)
  • R – resistance (Ω)

The most-used equation on the exam.

Resistance from a plot

R = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta I}
  • ΔV, ΔI – changes between two points of the I–V characteristic

Linear device = straight line; diode = nonlinear.

Power

P = \frac{W}{t} = V I = \frac{V^{2}}{R} = I^{2}R
  • P – power (W); 1 W = 1 J/s
  • W – energy (J); t – time (s)

Pick the form matching your two known quantities.

Energy & the kilowatt-hour

W = P t;\quad \text{kWh} = \frac{P(\text{W}) \cdot t(\text{h})}{1000}
  • W – energy (J, or kWh for billing)
  • P – power; t – time

Series, Parallel & Network Theorems Lecture 2

Series resistance

R_{T} = R_{1} + R_{2} + \dots + R_{N}
  • RT – total resistance (Ω); same current through every element

Parallel resistance

\frac{1}{R_{T}} = \frac{1}{R_{1}} + \dots + \frac{1}{R_{N}};\quad R_{T} = \frac{R_{1}R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}
  • RT < smallest branch; same voltage across every element

Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

\Sigma V = 0\quad (\text{closed loop})
  • sum of rises = sum of drops around any closed path

Voltage Divider Rule (VDR)

V_{x} = \frac{R_{x}E}{R_{T}}
  • Vx – voltage across Rx in a series string
  • E – applied voltage; RT – total series resistance

Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

\Sigma I_{\text{in}} = \Sigma I_{\text{out}}
  • at any node/junction; charge can't accumulate

Current Divider Rule (CDR)

I_{x} = \frac{R_{T}}{R_{x}}I_{T};\quad I_{1} = \frac{R_{2}I_{T}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}
  • IT – current entering the parallel set
  • two-branch form: other resistor on top

Smallest R carries the largest current.

Δ→Y conversion

R_{1} = \frac{R_{B}R_{C}}{R_{A}+R_{B}+R_{C}}
  • each Y resistor = product of the two adjacent Δ resistors ÷ sum of all three

Y→Δ conversion

R_{A} = \frac{R_{1}R_{2}+R_{1}R_{3}+R_{2}R_{3}}{R_{1}}
  • each Δ resistor = sum of pairwise products ÷ the opposite Y resistor

Source conversion

I = \frac{E}{R_{s}} \;\rightleftarrows\; E = I R_{s}
  • E series Rs ⇄ I parallel Rs; same Rs both ways

Thévenin ⇄ Norton

R_{N} = R_{\text{Th}},\quad I_{N} = \frac{E_{\text{Th}}}{R_{\text{Th}}}
  • ETh – open-circuit terminal voltage
  • IN – short-circuit terminal current
  • RTh – resistance with all sources zeroed (E→short, I→open)

Capacitors & RC Transients Lecture 3

Electric flux density

D = \frac{\Psi}{A},\quad \Psi = Q
  • D – flux density; Ψ – electric flux (C)
  • A – area the flux passes through

Capacitance (definition)

C = \frac{Q}{V}
  • C – capacitance (farads, F)
  • Q – stored charge (C); V – voltage (V)

Parallel-plate capacitance

C = \varepsilon \frac{A}{d},\quad \varepsilon = \varepsilon _{r}\varepsilon _{0}
  • A – plate area; d – separation
  • ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m; εr – relative permittivity of dielectric

RC time constant

\tau = R C
  • τ – time constant (s); transient ≈ done after 5τ
  • 63.2 % of the change happens in the first τ

RC charging

v_{C} = E(1 - e^{-t/\tau}),\quad i_{C} = \frac{E}{R}e^{-t/\tau}
  • E – supply voltage; τ = RC
  • vC: 0 → E; iC: E/R → 0

RC with initial conditions

v_{C} = V_{f} + (V_{i} - V_{f})e^{-t/\tau}
  • Vi – initial voltage; Vf – final (steady-state) voltage

Works for every first-order transient.

RC discharging

v_{C} = E e^{-t/\tau}
  • capacitor (charged to E) drains through R; current reverses direction

Capacitor current (defining law)

i_{C} = C \frac{dv_{C}}{dt}
  • steady DC → iC = 0 → capacitor = open circuit

Capacitors in series

Q_{T} = Q_{1} = Q_{2};\quad \frac{1}{C_{T}} = \frac{1}{C_{1}} + \frac{1}{C_{2}} + \dots
  • same charge on all; total capacitance shrinks (opposite of resistors)

Capacitors in parallel

Q_{T} = Q_{1} + Q_{2};\quad C_{T} = C_{1} + C_{2} + \dots
  • same voltage on all; capacitances add

Energy stored in a capacitor

W_{C} = \frac{C V^{2}}{2}
  • WC – energy (J) in the electric field
  • V – capacitor voltage (V)

Inductors & RL Transients Lecture 3

Magnetic flux density

B = \frac{\Phi}{A}
  • B – flux density (teslas, T = Wb/m²)
  • Φ – flux (webers, Wb); A – area (m²)

Permeability

\mu = \mu _{r}\mu _{0}
  • µ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ Wb/(A·m)
  • µr ≫ 1 for ferromagnetic cores (beware saturation)

Inductance of a coil

L = \frac{N^{2}\mu A}{l}
  • L – inductance (henries, H)
  • N – turns (squared!); A – core area; l – length

Faraday's law

e = N \frac{d\Phi}{dt}
  • e – induced voltage (V)
  • N – turns; dΦ/dt – rate of flux change (Wb/s)

Lenz: polarity opposes the change that caused it.

Inductor voltage (defining law)

v_{L} = L \frac{di_{L}}{dt}
  • steady DC → vL = 0 → inductor = short circuit

RL time constant & storage

\tau = \frac{L}{R};\quad i_{L} = \frac{E}{R}(1 - e^{-t/\tau}),\quad v_{L} = Ee^{-t/\tau}
  • current climbs to Im = E/R in ≈ 5τ; R in the denominator of τ

RL release-phase spike

v_{L} = -\left(1 + \frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\right)E
  • at the instant the switch opens; decays with τ′ = L/(R₁+R₂)
  • inductor current cannot change instantaneously

Inductors in series / parallel

L_{T} = L_{1}+L_{2}+\dots ;\quad \frac{1}{L_{T}} = \frac{1}{L_{1}}+\frac{1}{L_{2}}+\dots
  • combine exactly like resistors

Energy stored in an inductor

W_{L} = \frac{L I^{2}}{2}
  • WL – energy (J) in the magnetic field
  • I – steady current (A)

Sinusoidal Waveforms Lecture 4

Frequency & period

f = \frac{1}{T}
  • f – frequency (Hz); T – period (s)

Angular velocity

\omega = \frac{\alpha}{t} = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f
  • ω – angular velocity (rad/s); α – angle travelled (rad)
  • at 60 Hz: ω ≈ 377 rad/s

Degrees ⇄ radians

\text{rad} = \frac{\pi}{180^{\circ}}(\text{deg});\quad \text{deg} = \frac{180^{\circ}}{\pi}(\text{rad})
  • 2π rad = 360°; 1 rad ≈ 57.3°

General sinusoid

v = V_{m} \sin(\omega t \pm \theta )
  • Vm – peak value; ωt – running angle
  • +θ leads (peaks earlier), −θ lags

Average value

G = \frac{\text{algebraic area}}{\text{length of curve}}
  • area below the axis is negative
  • full sine cycle → average 0

RMS (effective) value

I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{I_{m}}{\sqrt{2}} = 0.707I_{m};\quad I_{m} = 1.414I_{\text{rms}}
  • same heating effect as the equal DC value (sinusoids only)
  • unlabelled AC ratings are RMS

Reactance, Impedance & Phasors Lectures 5–6

Inductive reactance

X_{L} = \omega L = 2\pi f L
  • XL – (Ω); grows linearly with f
  • vL leads iL by 90°

Capacitive reactance

X_{C} = \frac{1}{\omega C} = \frac{1}{2\pi f C}
  • XC – (Ω); shrinks as f grows
  • iC leads vC by 90°

Element impedances

\mathbf{Z}_{R} = R\angle 0^{\circ},\; \mathbf{Z}_{L} = X_{L}\angle 90^{\circ},\; \mathbf{Z}_{C} = X_{C}\angle -90^{\circ}
  • rectangular: ZL = jXL, ZC = −jXC

Series impedance

\mathbf{Z}_{T} = \mathbf{Z}_{1} + \mathbf{Z}_{2} + \dots ;\quad \text{RLC}: \mathbf{Z}_{T} = R + j(X_{L}-X_{C})
  • complex addition; XL and XC partially cancel

Parallel impedance

\frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}_{T}} = \frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}_{1}}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}_{2}}+\dots ;\quad \mathbf{Z}_{T} = \frac{\mathbf{Z}_{1}\mathbf{Z}_{2}}{\mathbf{Z}_{1}+\mathbf{Z}_{2}}
  • same form as parallel resistors, complex arithmetic

AC voltage divider

\mathbf{V}_{x} = \frac{\mathbf{Z}_{x}\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{Z}_{T}}
  • identical to DC VDR with impedances

AC current divider

\mathbf{I}_{1} = \frac{\mathbf{Z}_{2}\mathbf{I}_{T}}{\mathbf{Z}_{1}+\mathbf{Z}_{2}}
  • two-branch form; other branch's Z on top

Rectangular → polar

Z = \sqrt{X^{2}+Y^{2}},\quad \theta = \tan^{-1}\frac{Y}{X}
  • C = X + jY → Z∠θ; mind the quadrant if X < 0

Polar → rectangular

X = Z \cos\theta ,\quad Y = Z \sin\theta
  • add/subtract in rectangular; multiply/divide in polar

Maximum power transfer (AC)

Z_{L} = Z_{\text{Th}}^{*} \;\Rightarrow\; P_{\text{max}} = \frac{E_{\text{Th}}^{2}}{4R}
  • ZTh* – complex conjugate (same R, opposite X)
  • ETh – Thévenin (rms) voltage; R – its real part

AC Power Lecture 6

Average (real) power

P = \frac{V_{m}I_{m}}{2}\cos\theta = V_{\text{rms}}I_{\text{rms}} \cos\theta
  • P – watts (W); does real work
  • θ – angle between v and i

Apparent power

S = V I
  • S – volt-amperes (VA), with V, I in rms
  • what the source/wiring must be rated for

Reactive power

Q = V I \sin\theta
  • Q – volt-ampere reactive (VAR)
  • energy exchanged with L/C fields, not consumed

Power triangle

S^{2} = P^{2} + Q^{2}
  • P along the real axis, QL up / QC down, S the hypotenuse

Power factor

F_{p} = \cos\theta = \frac{P}{S}
  • 1 = purely resistive, 0 = purely reactive
  • lagging = inductive; leading = capacitive

Correction: parallel C cancels QL → Fp → 1.

Resonance Lecture 7

Resonance condition (series)

X_{L} = X_{C} \;\Rightarrow\; \mathbf{Z}_{T_{s}} = R
  • impedance minimum, current maximum (I = E/R), circuit purely resistive

Resonant frequency

f_{s} = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{L C}},\quad \omega _{s} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L C}}
  • L (H), C (F) → fs (Hz)
  • parallel (high-Q): fp ≈ same formula

Quality factor (series)

Q_{s} = \frac{X_{L}}{R} = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{L/C}
  • dimensionless; high Q = sharp, selective resonance

Resonant voltage rise

V_{L_{s}} = V_{C_{s}} = Q_{s}E
  • element voltages = Q × source voltage (can be hundreds of volts!)

Bandwidth

\text{BW} = f_{2} - f_{1} = \frac{R}{2\pi L} = \frac{f_{s}}{Q_{s}}
  • f₁, f₂ – half-power frequencies (I = 0.707 Imax)
  • also fs = √(f₁f₂)

Parallel resonance highlights

Z_{T} \text{max},\quad I \text{min},\quad \text{BW} = \frac{f_{p}}{Q_{p}}
  • tank (L ∥ C): impedance peaks at resonance; Qp ≈ coil's Ql for high-Q circuits

Transformers Lecture 7

Mutual inductance

M = k\sqrt{L_{p}L_{s}}
  • M – mutual inductance (H)
  • k = Φm/Φp – coupling coefficient (0…1)

Induced secondary voltage

e_{s} = M \frac{di_{p}}{dt}
  • changing primary current induces secondary voltage (and vice versa: ep = M dis/dt)

Turns ratio relations

a = \frac{N_{p}}{N_{s}};\quad \frac{E_{p}}{E_{s}} = a;\quad \frac{I_{p}}{I_{s}} = \frac{1}{a}
  • Np, Ns – turns; voltage scales with turns, current inversely

Reflected impedance

Z_{p} = a^{2}Z_{L}
  • load ZL on the secondary appears a² times larger at the primary
  • basis of impedance matching (8:1 → 8 Ω looks like 512 Ω)

Coupled coils in series

L_{T} = L_{1} + L_{2} \pm 2M
  • +2M fluxes aiding, −2M opposing
